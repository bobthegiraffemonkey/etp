Motivation
==========
Gimmick where there's several versions of the room, with multiple toggles to overlay one or more of them over the base room at once. These interact in interesting ways. Must be simple enough to reason about that a typical guest could escape!


Narrative
=========
You shared a physics class with someone at uni. Bit eccentric and very clever. Likes colours, wore colourful and patterned things a lot. Used to hang out a bit.

A few years after uni, her brother calls you (start room by saying 'ring ring' and having a call). Seems to be missing. Might have been just before the problem with the substation in the area. Probably they lost some work and are distracted fixing it, forgot to charge their phone or their power is still down. You live near-ish her lab, can you check in?

Lab is colour based (Spectral Overlay Labs), but when you go in it's mostly plain white (more colours in the lobby?). Toggles are rgb buttons. Hidden controls to put in an a hex code at the end to complete the room - this overlays some weird experimental box (experimental safety deposit box technology) that the scientist got trapped in when the power surged and the controls got into a bad state. End room by switching to normal lights to wake her up, after removing her from the box thingy.


Gimmick rules
=============
- Four toggles
  - W: on = "magic" lights, off = normal lights.
  - R, G, B = toggle that colour on/off when magic lights are on.
- Objects (including ink) have colours or R, G, or B (no mixtures).
- When the magic lights include the colour of an object, it is included (overlaid) in the W room.
- If object A exists in a position that would overlap with object B, and object A is included but B is not, then turning on the appropriate light to make B appear will superimpose A and B into one solid object. The colour of the overlap is the result of adding the colours (like how RGB works on screens).
  - E.g. red light on, green object would overlap with a red object, then turning on the green light will merge those two objects. The overlap would be yellow.
- Hex control will only load in other room if the colour is an exact match, so with the solution colour in place you only see the box and none of the other items.
- Mixes of two colours would be possible, but maybe too tricky to make a good puzzle that's not too confusing?
